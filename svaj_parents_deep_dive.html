Satisfaction & engagement

Satisfaction scores: parents vs. no-kids (avg 1–5)
Areas where the two groups differ most
Parents rate lifecycle event support notably lower (4.36 vs 4.68). B'nei mitzvah prep, bar/bat mitzvah planning, and family milestones may not feel as supported as empty-nesters expect.
Engagement frequency: parents vs. no-kids (avg 1–5)
1 = rarely/never · 5 = weekly
Parents attend Shabbat davening significantly less (3.68 vs 4.29) and shiurim far less (1.69 vs 2.20). This isn't disengagement — it's logistics. Kids' schedules are suppressing participation across the board.

The new parent integration gap

Belonging, integration & welcoming scores — parents by how long they've been at SVAJ
New parent families feel meaningfully less integrated than established ones — by nearly 1 full point
Integration score: 3.58 (new parents) vs. 4.53 (long-term parents). That's a 0.95-point gap. These are the families SVAJ most needs to retain — young, with kids who'll be in the community for decades — and right now they don't feel fully embedded. The welcoming score also lags (3.92 vs. 4.38). Belonging catches up over time, but integration is the sharper risk and the earlier warning signal.

Strategic implications for the planning committee

1

Youth programming is a retention lever, not just a satisfaction gap

Parents explicitly say families leave SVAJ for other shuls specifically for kids' social and programming experiences. This isn't a satisfaction score problem — it's competitive attrition. Every family lost to youth programming takes 20+ years of future membership with it. The ROI on fixing this is enormous.

2

Fix childcare logistics to unlock participation

38% of parents cite childcare logistics as a direct barrier — the highest rate of any segment. Toddler programming at nap time. No childcare at adult events. These are structural, solvable operational problems. A Shabbat morning childcare option alone could meaningfully shift engagement numbers for this cohort.

3

New parent onboarding deserves its own track

New parents (≤3 yrs) score 3.58 on integration vs. 4.53 for long-term families. General newcomer onboarding isn't reaching this group effectively — they need parent-specific connection points: a families-with-kids Shabbat lunch, a new-parent WhatsApp group, existing parent mentors. The belonging instinct is there; the infrastructure isn't.

4

Integrate teens into the main shul — not parallel tracks

Multiple parents independently named the same idea: teens leading davening in the main shul, not a separate teen minyan. The instinct is sound — teens build identity through participation. A separate minyan implicitly tells them SVAJ isn't fully for them yet. That lesson sticks through college and beyond.

5

Design learning for parents' constraints, not their interests

Parents' adult education priority score is 3.11 vs. 4.38 for non-parents — not because they don't care about learning, but because they can't show up on Thursday nights. Short, Shabbat-integrated, family-friendly learning experiences (a 20-minute parsha discussion at kiddush; a monthly Sunday morning learning breakfast with childcare) will reach this cohort when traditional formats won't.
