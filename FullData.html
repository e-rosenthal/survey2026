ABSOLUTELY! The discussions at board meetings in the fall over the Kol Sasson lease made many of my friends feel personally held in contempt. Executive board members have no accountability for the social choices they make: when they hurt feelings, they don't have to apologize. Instead of being held accountable for sound business choices that have unfortunately had the side effect of hurting certain people very deeply, the people in charge are not encouraged to do cheshbon nefesh or sent a shaliach to make peace--they are covered up for and protected, to give the general impression that some Skokie Valley members are more equal than others. Rav Ari is forced into all sort of uncomfortable positions trying to make everyone feel welcome when he wasn't the one who snubbed them to begin with. And if, God forbid, it's Rav Ari who inadvertently made the mistake, no one is there supporting him in trying to make peace. Planning a simcha through Carin Netter in the front office is not an experience I intend to repeat. She was uniformly overworked, undersupported, hassled, harried, stressed, didactic, pessimistic, and nay-saying. We could do no right by her, and she never once said anything kind or supportive to any member of the family. She was very quick to forbid us outright from using the space in any way that required staff support, and treated us and our simcha like a personal inconvenience. Not once did anyone say anything to acknowledge that the date was chosen by outside sources and we could not move it even if we tried; not once did anyone acknowledge our efforts to move the date (impossible) or the location--we literally had no other choice than to celebrate at Skokie Valley. Not once did Carin ever say "mazal tov to your family on this life event," via email or in person. I understand that the building is crowded: we will be sure to do our share to alleviate the problem b celebrating all of our life cycle events elsewhere hereafter.
